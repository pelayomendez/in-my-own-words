# Source index

The audit trail. Every trait in the profile traces back to a row here — that is the rule that keeps a profile from drifting into whatever the model thinks good writing looks like.

In a real profile these rows point at files in `sources/raw/`. This example is compiled from widely documented characteristics rather than from ingested text, which is why the source column names works rather than filenames. Yours should not look like this.

| Date | Source | Lang | What the profile learned |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1963 | *Confessions of an Advertising Man* | en | Assertion-first structure; numbered rules as the unit of argument; the reader treated as intelligent; failures reported by name |
| 1983 | *Ogilvy on Advertising* | en | Declarative headings that state the finding; research as the arbiter; precise figures over adjectives; the imperative close |
| various | Internal agency memos | en | Short sentences and hard breaks; plain Anglo-Saxon vocabulary punctured by one patrician word a page; contempt for jargon as an explicit stance |
| various | Long-copy print advertisements | en | The single-sentence paragraph used as a hammer at the end of an evidence run; concrete trade nouns; no intensifiers |

## Not covered

- **Any language but English.** No lexicon exists for another language and none should be invented — his register is inseparable from mid-century transatlantic English.
- **Spoken register.** No transcripts. Interviews may differ substantially from the written voice.
