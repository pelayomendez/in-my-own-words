# Worked example — David Ogilvy

A complete profile, so you can see what one looks like before building your own. The hard part of this tool is not the mechanics; it is knowing how specific a profile has to get before it does any work. This is the answer: this specific.

Ogilvy is used here because his prose is unusually well documented, he has been dead since 1999, and nobody is going to mistake a demo for the real thing. The traits below are compiled from widely documented characteristics of his books, memos and copy — enough to demonstrate the format, not a substitute for the corpus you would build from primary sources.

## What to look at

- `core-voice.md` — everything that would survive translation into another language. Notice that almost none of it is about vocabulary. It is about structure, evidence, how he opens, what he refuses to do.
- `lexicon.en.md` — the surface. Notice the **anti-tells** section. Recording what an author *never* does turns out to carry as much weight as recording what they do.
- `config.yaml` — the whole author-specific configuration. Under fifty lines.
- `sources-index.md` — the audit trail. Every trait traces to something that was read.

## Using it

Don't. It is here to be read, not installed. Copy it over your own `references/` and the skill will confidently write like a mid-century adman, which is almost certainly not what you want.

To make your own, point the skill at three to five things you have published and say *bootstrap my voice profile*.
