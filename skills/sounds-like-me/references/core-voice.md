# Core voice — language-agnostic

**Status: empty.** No corpus has been ingested. Run Bootstrap — point the skill at three to five things you have published and say *bootstrap my voice profile*. It will replace this file with observed traits.

A filled-in example to read first: [`examples/ogilvy/core-voice.md`](../../../examples/ogilvy/core-voice.md).

---

This layer holds only what **survives translation**: structure, rhythm, argument moves, what earns emphasis, how a piece opens and lands. Words, punctuation, register and idiom go in `lexicon.<lang>.md` instead. Getting that boundary wrong is what makes output in a second language read like translated prose.

Fill these sections. Every trait carries a verbatim quotation from the corpus — a trait with no evidence is the model's taste leaking into your profile.

## Shape

Target length. How many movements, in what order. Where the thesis lands.

## Headings

Their grammatical form, not their topics. Noun phrase, declarative, imperative, question? Are there any at all?

## Rhythm

Words per paragraph — measured, not estimated. Never count rendered lines. If the author has more than one register, say what decides which one a piece takes, or the profile will happily approve a piece written in the wrong one.

Note any signature rhythmic move and how often it recurs.

## Argument moves

The recurring rhetorical shapes: the reframe, the concession, the reversal, the appeal to evidence. This is the deepest layer and the one most worth getting right — it is what makes a piece feel like the author *thinking*, rather than sounding like them.

Give a frequency for anything that becomes a tic when overused.

## Metaphor discipline

How many per piece. Sustained or incidental. Which source domains. A governing metaphor recurs across sections and supplies the closing image; an incidental analogy appears once and never returns.

## Evidence

Named sources or vague attribution. Precise numbers or round ones. Links. What the author reaches for when they need to prove something.

## Voice and stance

Person. How the reader is addressed, and where. Hedging calibration — firm on what, tentative on what. Humour. What the author refuses to do.

## Level of polish

Do typos survive? Is the copy edited hard or published warm? This matters: prose that is cleaner than the author's own reads as much less like them than prose with one rough edge.

## Closing pattern

How pieces end. This and the opening are the highest-signal sentences in any corpus — quote several of each verbatim.
